# Emerging Threat Watchlist

## Purpose

This document provides a consolidated view of emerging financial crime threats currently monitored within the Emerging Threat Intelligence Library.

The objective is to track threat maturity, intelligence confidence, potential impact and analytical readiness.

The watchlist supports prioritisation, horizon scanning and future analytics planning.

---

## Threat Status Framework

| Status | Definition |
|----------|------------|
| Emerging | Early indicators identified with limited operational evidence |
| Active | Threat observed in multiple jurisdictions or intelligence sources |
| Escalating | Threat increasing in frequency, sophistication or impact |
| Mainstream | Established financial crime typology requiring operational controls |
| Archived | Threat no longer considered material or has merged into another category |

## Trigger Events

The following developments should trigger immediate threat reassessment:

### Regulatory Triggers

- New FATF publications
- New FCA thematic reviews
- New OFAC or OFSI advisories
- New FinCEN advisories

### Criminal Intelligence Triggers

- Europol threat assessments
- NCA strategic assessments
- Egmont typology publications

### Technology Triggers

- Major AI advancements
- New stablecoin adoption trends
- Emerging digital identity technologies
- Significant embedded finance growth

### Geopolitical Triggers

- New sanctions programmes
- Major geopolitical conflicts
- Trade restrictions
- Significant supply chain disruption

## Strategic Assessment

The current threat landscape is increasingly shaped by the convergence of:

- Artificial Intelligence
- Digital Assets
- Geopolitical Instability
- Sanctions Expansion
- Identity Fraud
- Financial System Transformation

Future analytical investment should focus on threats where:

- Intelligence confidence is increasing
- Impact is potentially significant
- Existing controls are immature
- Detection capabilities remain limited

The watchlist provides the operational mechanism for tracking these developments and prioritising future analytical responses.
