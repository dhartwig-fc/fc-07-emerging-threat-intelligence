# Future Scenarios

## Purpose

This document explores plausible future financial crime scenarios arising from geopolitical, technological, regulatory and economic change.

The objective is to understand how emerging threats may evolve and what future analytics capabilities may be required.

---

## Scenario 1 – AI Generated Trade Documentation

### Scenario

Generative AI enables the large-scale creation of realistic invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin and shipping documentation.

### Criminal Adaptation

Criminal groups use AI-generated documents to support false trade activity, disguise TBML schemes and bypass manual document reviews.

### Linked Threats

- ET001 – AI Generated Trade Documents
- ET005 – Trade Finance Sanctions Evasion
- ET009 – Supply Chain Finance Manipulation

### Required Capabilities

- Document similarity analytics
- Metadata analysis
- AI-assisted document validation
- Trade documentation risk scoring

---

## Scenario 2 – Stablecoin Cross-Border Settlement

### Scenario

Stablecoins become increasingly common as cross-border settlement mechanisms.

### Criminal Adaptation

Criminal networks use stablecoins to move value quickly across jurisdictions outside traditional banking rails.

### Linked Threats

- ET003 – Stablecoin Layering
- ET004 – Crypto Trade Finance Laundering

### Required Capabilities

- Wallet analytics
- Blockchain monitoring
- Stablecoin flow analysis
- Digital asset risk scoring

---

## Scenario 3 – Synthetic Identity Networks

### Scenario

Digital onboarding and remote account opening continue to expand.

### Criminal Adaptation

Criminal groups create synthetic identity networks using shared attributes, fake documentation and linked devices.

### Linked Threats

- ET002 – Synthetic Identity Networks
- ET010 – Deepfake Identity Fraud

### Required Capabilities

- Entity resolution
- Device intelligence
- Identity risk scoring
- Network analytics

---

## Scenario 4 – Geopolitical Sanctions Evasion

### Scenario

Geopolitical conflict drives additional sanctions, export controls and trade restrictions.

### Criminal Adaptation

Sanctioned actors use front companies, transshipment hubs and complex ownership structures to evade restrictions.

### Linked Threats

- ET005 – Trade Finance Sanctions Evasion
- ET009 – Supply Chain Finance Manipulation

### Required Capabilities

- Trade route analytics
- Ownership network analysis
- Sanctions exposure scoring
- Supply chain intelligence

---

## Scenario 5 – Open Banking Mule Networks

### Scenario

Open Banking, faster payments and digital payment ecosystems increase transaction speed and account connectivity.

### Criminal Adaptation

Criminals recruit and coordinate mule networks to rapidly move fraud proceeds through connected accounts.

### Linked Threats

- ET006 – Open Banking Mule Networks
- ET007 – Social Media Mule Recruitment

### Required Capabilities

- Payment velocity analytics
- Mule account detection
- Shared device analysis
- Network-based risk scoring

---

## Strategic Conclusion

Future financial crime risk is likely to be driven by the convergence of:

- Geopolitical instability
- Digital assets
- Artificial intelligence
- Faster payments
- Synthetic identities
- Complex ownership networks

Financial crime analytics must therefore evolve from static rules toward adaptive, intelligence-led and network-aware detection capabilities.
