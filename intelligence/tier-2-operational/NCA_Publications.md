## Executive Summary

The National Crime Agency (NCA) provides the primary law enforcement intelligence picture of serious and organised crime impacting the United Kingdom.

The NCA's strategic assessments consistently identify money laundering, fraud, organised criminal networks, sanctions evasion and illicit finance as major threats to UK national security and economic stability.

The NCA assesses that it is a realistic possibility that more than £100 billion is laundered through or within the UK, or through UK corporate structures, every year.  [oai_citation:0‡National Crime Agency](https://www.nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk/news/nca-and-fca-publish-priorities-to-combat-biggest-economic-crime-threats?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

NCA intelligence increasingly highlights the convergence of:

- Organised crime
- Fraud
- Digital assets
- Sanctions evasion
- International criminal networks
- Professional money laundering
- Technology-enabled crime

The NCA provides a highly operational view of how criminal networks adapt, evolve and exploit the financial system.  [oai_citation:1‡National Crime Agency](https://www.nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk/what-we-do/crime-threats/money-laundering-and-illicit-finance?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Repository Implications

NCA intelligence reinforces the need for:

- Network intelligence
- Intelligence-led investigations
- Entity resolution
- Cross-border risk monitoring
- Trade analytics
- Fraud analytics
- Digital asset monitoring

The NCA consistently demonstrates that modern financial crime is increasingly networked, international and enabled by technology.

Traditional transaction monitoring alone is unlikely to identify sophisticated organised criminal activity. Effective future controls will increasingly require:

- Graph Analytics
- Relationship Discovery
- Behavioural Analytics
- Intelligence-Led Detection
- AI-Assisted Investigation
